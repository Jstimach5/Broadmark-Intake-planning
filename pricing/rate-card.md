## Surcharges (stack them — they reflect real cost)

- **Steep or rocky ground: +25–50%** (rock cuts tooth life 10×; industry-standard surcharge)
- **Heavy material (frequent 6–8″ stems): +25%** — the HM418 is rated to 8″ but the sweet
  spot is ≤6″; 8″+ stems get chainsaw-felled first, billed T&M, or declined
- **Wet ground:** reschedule by default; +25% if the customer insists and it's workable.
  This will come up constantly — the Sumpter/Willow area runs to wet clay and swamp
  ground; walk any low parcel before quoting firm
- **Hidden wire/junk discovered mid-job:** stop, photo, change-order at the hourly rate —
  put "site must be disclosed; concealed metal/rock billed hourly" in quote terms

## Add-ons (upsell menu on every quote)

- Finish/back-drag second pass for a lawn-ready look: +10–15%
- Chainsaw pre-fell of oversize trees: T&M ($125/hr labor)
- **Regrowth maintenance plan: $500–1,500/visit, annually or every other year** —
  mulching doesn't kill roots; regrowth in 6–12 months is guaranteed (autumn olive and
  buckthorn especially). This is the recurring-revenue engine: pitch it AT the
  walkthrough, not later
- Herbicide follow-up: refer/partner unless you get a Michigan applicator license

## Discount rules

**Allowed (structural discounts — cost actually drops):**
- Multi-day (saved mobilization)
- Neighbor bundling (2+ properties same trip: waive the second mobilization)
- Fill-in scheduling ("I can flex you into a cancellation for 10% off")
- Repeat/annual maintenance customers
- Winter fill work (Dec–Feb, frozen ground): up to 10–15% off to keep the machine busy
  in the slow season — frozen ground is actually good mulching ground

**Never:**
- Matching a brush-hog or rental-machine quote (different product — say so)
- Cash-discount pressure
- "The other guy is cheaper" without a written scope match
- Anything that takes the effective rate below **$190/hr** (the walk-away floor)

## Red flags — decline, hourly-only, or refer out

Rock outcrops visible • old interior fence lines buried in brush • dumped
junk/appliances in the vegetation • majority of stems >8″ • standing water/swamp •
slopes that feel sketchy on the walk • customer wants "roots gone / build-ready pad"
(that's grubbing — wrong tool, refer it out) • price-only shoppers at bush-hog budgets •
"just bill me whatever it takes" with no scope agreement.

**Michigan-specific:** clearing in **regulated wetlands requires an EGLE permit** — and
SE Michigan (Sumpter especially) is full of them. If a parcel is wet year-round, has
cattails/standing water, or the customer mentions "the county said…", do not touch it
without the customer producing the permit. Declining these jobs protects the business;
the fines land on whoever ran the machine.
